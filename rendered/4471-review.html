Parser failure

The shared parser reads month first for tenants whose locale writes day first. For example, R-41200 from Harbour Dental (en-GB, booking-v2, app) describes 7 April becoming 4 July. R-41205 belongs to Morrow Tax (fr-FR), even though its browser locale is en-US and intake calls it hint: the tenant’s configuration matters.

Reproduce by passing 07/04/2026 as a day-first tenant’s date through the shared app/email parser. Expected: 7 April. Reported: 4 July. The export describes this behavior; it does not contain the product executable, so this is a proposed reproduction, not a test we ran. Make parsing follow an explicit tenant date contract. Check ambiguous dates, unambiguous day-first dates, month-first tenants, and locale switching before closing the ticket.

Form hint failure

booking-v3 prints MM/DD/YYYY but reads the form in a day-first tenant’s order. This is not the same failure as the shared parser. Copper Studio, Alder Physio, and Pale Harbour use that form. Their app/email reports still follow the parser path. R-41230 is a Copper Studio form report tagged parser by intake, but the tenant/form combination places it in the hint group.

Reproduce in a day-first booking-v3 form: follow the month-first hint and enter 01/06/2026 intending 6 January. Expected: 6 January. With day-first reading: 1 June. Make the visible hint and validation agree with the tenant’s date contract; test the form separately from the app/email parser. A parser-only fix cannot establish that the hint is fixed.

Unresolved observations are not a proven third cause

The desk notes identify reports that the classifier files as parser but whose bodies do not demonstrate a month/day swap. One describes different days for Lisbon and Toronto; another says an invite arrived before the booking. Keep these in a separate investigation ticket rather than calling them confirmed parser failures or declaring a timezone bug proved.

Obtain the original entered date, event identifier, stored instant, timezone, all-day flag, invite payload, and creation/delivery timestamps. Compare local-date preservation and UTC conversion, including DST boundaries, without assuming they explain every report. R-41236 needs a separate chronology check within this investigation. Split it further if evidence establishes independent mechanisms. Reassign each report once, preserving its ID and audit trail.

Replace subject-only triage

The accepted decision 0001 remains an historical record; this proposed review does not silently edit or declare it superseded. Its subject-only rule can merge different failures and separate duplicates of the same failure. The executable sameCause function instead checks tenant locale, form, and channel. Even that rule needs a body-level exception for contradictory observations.

Proposed triage: deduplicate export rows by report ID, inspect the body and tenant’s configuration, and group by supported failure mechanism. Keep uncertain reports in investigation. Similar subjects, browser locales, or intake tags alone are not sufficient evidence for merging. Seek desk approval for a new decision record before replacing the accepted operational policy.
